Policy Servicing FAQ

Last updated: 10 Feb 2026

What happens if I miss my premium due date?

You have a 31-day grace period from the due date. Your coverage remains in full force during the grace period. If the premium is still unpaid after the grace period, the policy lapses and coverage stops.

My policy lapsed. Can I still bring it back?

Yes. You may apply to reinstate a lapsed policy within two years from the lapse date. You will need to pay all unpaid premiums with interest, and you may be asked to submit evidence of insurability. Reinstatement is subject to underwriting approval and is not automatic.

Can I change how often I pay my premium?

Yes. You may change your payment mode to annual, semi-annual, quarterly, or monthly on your policy anniversary. The total annual premium differs by mode, so ask for a computation before deciding.

Do my riders stop if the policy lapses?

Yes. All attached riders, including accidental death benefit, critical illness, waiver of premium, and hospital income benefit, lapse together with the base policy. Riders cannot be reinstated on their own.

How do I change my beneficiary?

Submit a change-of-beneficiary form through any branch or the client portal. A revocable beneficiary may be changed at any time. An irrevocable beneficiary can only be changed with that beneficiary's written consent.

How long does payment posting take?

Over-the-counter bank payments post within two banking days. E-wallet and online transfers post within one banking day. Official receipts are emailed and are available in the client portal.

What happens if I miss my premium due date?

You have a 31-day grace period from the due date. Your coverage remains in full force during the grace period. If the premium is still unpaid after the grace period, the policy lapses and coverage stops.

Is my policy covered if I travel abroad?

Life coverage continues while you are abroad. Some riders have territorial limits, so check your policy contract or ask a licensed advisor about your specific riders.
