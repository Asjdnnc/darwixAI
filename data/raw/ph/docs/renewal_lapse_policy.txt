1. PREMIUM DUE DATES
Premiums may be paid annually, semi-annually, quarterly, or monthly. The due
date stated on the policy contract applies to every mode. Changing the payment
mode is allowed on the policy anniversary and may change the total annual
premium.

2. GRACE PERIOD
Every policy has a grace period of thirty-one (31) days from the premium due
date. Coverage stays in full force during the grace period. If the premium
remains unpaid at the end of the grace period, the policy lapses.

3. WHAT LAPSE MEANS FOR THE CLIENT
When a policy lapses, life coverage and all attached riders stop. A claim filed
after the lapse date will be denied. Any cash value may be applied automatically
under the non-forfeiture provision if the policy has been in force long enough.

4. REINSTATEMENT
A lapsed policy may be reinstated within two (2) years from the lapse date. The
client must settle all unpaid premiums with interest and may be required to
submit evidence of insurability. Reinstatement is not automatic and is subject
to underwriting approval.

5. RIDERS
Riders attached to the base policy lapse together with the base policy. Common
riders are accidental death benefit, critical illness, waiver of premium, and
hospital income benefit. Riders cannot be reinstated separately from the base
policy.

6. BENEFICIARY
A policyholder may change a revocable beneficiary at any time by submitting the
change-of-beneficiary form. An irrevocable beneficiary cannot be changed without
the written consent of that beneficiary. Beneficiary records are confidential
and are never discussed with anyone other than the policyholder.

7. WHAT AGENTS AND AUTOMATED CALLS MAY NOT DO
Agents must not threaten the client, must not state that coverage has already
been cancelled while the policy is still within the grace period, must not
guarantee reinstatement, and must not quote a reinstatement amount without a
computation from Policy Administration.
